Tips for Pistol Shooting Cross-Dominant without a Blinder

May Chan // June 5, 2026

Some personal tips and experiences around shooting without any eyewear.

Shooting without any eyewear (aside from prescription contacts, which I wear in my daily life) was a decision I made when I first started training the pistol. I did try shooting with a blinder early on, but found both that I didn't like wearing shooting glasses and that the blinder itself was somewhat distracting.

It so happens that I am right handed, and cross-eye dominant, which means when I hold up the sights the vision of my left eye takes precedence over my right eye. If I were to shoot using my left eye to line up the sights, I would need to cant my pistol at a fairly extreme angle, and/or hold the pistol at an awkward angle.

Even at the beginning I was of the mindset that one should strive to shoot in as natural and relaxed a position as possible, so shooting with my cross-dominant eye was out of the question. Rather, I saw it as a challenge to figure out if I could shoot well with my non-dominant eye.

Currently, I am completely comfortable shooting with my non-dominant eye. Not only that, but I also prefer to shoot without blocking my left eye. It's hard to explain, but even though I'm using the right eye to align the sights, it still feels like my left eye is contributing, somehow.

Shooting cross-eye dominant with no blinder

The biggest challenge to shooting on your non-dominant eye is training yourself to look at the sights using the non-dominant eye. For simplicity's sake, I'll consider the dominant side to be the right side in the following examples.

Forcing cross-dominant eye sight alignment: Hold up the sights, then close your left eye (or block it with your left). Align the sights with your right eye, then open or unblock your left eye so both eyes are open. You should see double, but the sights will still be lined up with your right eye.

It's quite normal for your left eye to take over at this point, causing you to lose the sight alignment. That's okay, we're training the brain to use the non-dominant eye. Repeat until you can maintain sight alignment with your right eye long enough to take the shot.

With cross-eye dominance you will always see double. Two sights and two targets. For myself, even after almost two years of shooting I still see double, though I've read online that people have switched which eye is dominant over time somehow.

The beginning is probably the most frustrating part of the process, especially when the brain overrides the right eye, but I managed to get over that hurdle myself after just two or three days of practice.

Tip:Resist the urge to keep the left eye closed or blocked. If you're going to do that, you might as well shoot with a proper blinder

Tip:If you raise the gun and you're not sure if you're aligned with the correct eye, close or block your left eye briefly and see if the sight alignment is still correct.

Tips for dealing with blurred vision:

Try:

  • Crossing your eyes or letting your vision lose focus as you raise the gun, then focus specifically on the front sight as you bring the sights into alignment above the target
  • Looking at something far in the distance, then focus on something close by, before lifting the gun
  • Stay hydrated. Water has a huge effect on vision.

Miscellaneous Tips and Thoughts:

  • It's of utmost importance to keep the muscles on the face as relaxed as possible, so you should not squint either eye at all when aiming.
  • Shooting without a blinder is completely optional. I don't believe I gain any benefit from it from a performance perspective. More than likely there's a downside to not using eyewear, but so far I've been able to perform well enough, so I haven't felt the need to change.
  • I strongly believe that you should shoot with your eye and hand on the same side. Cross eye-hand shooting adds additional stress and strange angles to the whole equation that add additional stress on the body that will hurt consistency. Obviously you can train to mitigate those issues, and personal preference is a big factor. But I think this is particularly important to get right at the beginning when you pick up the sport.
