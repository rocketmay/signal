Maintaining Top Physical Condition

May Chan // October 12, 2026

If you're training on a regular basis, you will notice that your physical condition has a big impact on your day to day performance. This article explores some factors that affect your physical condition to help you stay at your peak.

Factors that affect your performance

These are listed in rough order of importance, according to my own experiences.

Sleep Quality

Sleep quality and quantity has a massive effect on your performance and so is number one on the list. You cannot perform well if you don't get good sleep. This shouldn't come as a surprise (or if it does, there are plenty of resources online that talk about the necessity of good sleep for performance), so to keep things short I'll say this: If you want to perform well consistently, make sure you do your best to get a good night sleep every night.

Hydration

Drinking water is second on the list. Not only should you be drinking water consistently throughout the day, but you should also have water on the line with you when you train and compete.

In addition to all the regular effects of staying hydrated, precision shooting is particularly affected by dehydration because it directly affects the sharpness of your vision. When you are dehydrated, the outer layers of your eye (the tear film) become unstable and irregular, causing your sight to blur.

I keep eyedrops in my bag specifically if I feel like my eyes are dry, but you definitely want to prevent your eyes from feeling dry or irritated in the first place.

Anecdotally, taking regular small sips of water during practice and matches keeps me feeling sharp, alert and in good form.

Hunger and Nutrition

There is a lot of literature, both health and sports related, on proper diet and nutrition, so I won't go into it. Suffice to say, a healthy body is a performant body.

Stimulants

Coupled with nutrition, stimulants deserve a special mention. Caffeine in particular is a vice that many of us in the modern day share (yes, me included). However, caffeine has a number of effects that are particularly bad for this sport:

  • Elevated heart rate. This is a big one. Consuming caffeine causes your heart to beat faster, which has a huge effect on the stability of your hold.
  • Dehydration. Caffeine is a mild diuretic, which makes staying hydrated extra important.
  • Affects Sleep. Caffeine affects the quality of your deep sleep and lasts in your system for up to eight hours. So you might want to rethink your afternoon coffees and teas.

I strictly avoid caffeine before I train, which is why I prefer training in the mornings. But I have tried training after (or even while) drinking coffee, just to judge the effects for myself. I encourage you to do your own trials and make the decision for yourself.

Overall Fatigue

If you're tired or exhausted, it's difficult to perform well. While we all have different life circumstances, if you can plan your training such that you are as fresh and awake as possible on any given day, your performance will be more consistent. This is why I prefer to train in the morning, since work can often be draining.

If I have a particularly tiring or exhausting day, I do still try to get my training in, but I do my best to manage my schedule to not train tired. Oftentimes a quick twenty minute power nap is enough to charge me up for the evening session.

Eyesight

Take care of your eyes! This sport definitely relies on good eye health. Make sure you read in good lighting, don't stare at your bright screen in bed in the dark, and all the other things that keep your eyes happy.

Stress

Dealing with stress is more on the mental side and so won't be covered in this article, but I will note that being under stress (of any kind) does take a physical toll on your body.

This point is mostly here to point out the physical affect of stress on the body and start building awareness of it, rather than specifically saying to avoid it (don't we all wish it were that easy!).

Incidental Factors

Exercise Fatigue

This article isn't about exercising, but if you have a regular training routine (which you should), you are familiar with soreness that comes from training. There's a lot that can be said about training, but for the purposes of this article, be aware that if you're doing them back to back, the physical training probably should be done after your shooting practice for the day, rather than before.

Strain and Injury

If you've strained a muscle or suffered an injury, it will have an effect on your training. No one wants to be injured, but if you're serious about your performance in shooting, you definitely want to avoid risky activities.

Illness

When you're sick, rest up and recover as quick as you can. Training while sick will hurt more than it helps.

Alcohol and other… vices

Obviously, none of us are drinking while shooting, but hangovers aren't going to do you any favours in training. Insert your specific vice and its side effect here.

Developing your self-awareness

A lot of the above factors are under your control, but also there are a lot of factors that aren't under your control. The big list above is not meant to be an exhaustive list, nor is it some kind of underhanded plea for you to live life in a safe plastic bubble where nothing bad happens to you and you make the mature adult choice every time.

Instead, it's meant to get you thinking about what kinds of things affect your physical condition. A lot of us just take life as it comes, doing things without thinking about how exactly it affects our performance. Take this opportunity to contemplate the above list, and maybe add a few of your own, and really evaluate them in the context of your own training.

Developing this kind of awareness will help you sidestep a lot of problems. The more in tune with your body you are, the better you can manage your condition. You can start to take control of your choices, rather than let those choices happen on rails.

Choose your poison

Again, life is complicated, and no one is perfect. I enjoy my daily coffee and my deep fried chicken, and I will sometimes consume them knowing that it's going to cause problems in my training. But what's important is I'm acknowledging the decision and its impact.

Since I know how heavily poor sleep quality and dehydration affects my training, I make an extra effort to make sure I go to sleep on time, even if I'm tempted to binge a new show I'm hooked on. I keep a water bottle next to me throughout the day and have one by my bed, and build the habits to keep myself properly hydrated.

Yeah, I have days that are particularly stressful or exhausting. When I train after a day like that, being aware of it helps me to stay focused and evaluate my performance accordingly. Or, if I think that my performance will be significantly impacted, then I give myself permission to skip training on that day, and make up for it at a later date.

At the end of the day, being aware of the things that affect your performance and making choices or taking action to maximize your condition will enable you to maintain it on a consistent basis, allowing you to make the most of every session.
